Public-tree governance

Consent, soil safety, canopy protection and long-term ownership of outcomes.

Governance by consent—not guerrilla conversion

1. Municipal approval

Map ownership, bylaws, tree protection and pruning rules. Never alter council-owned street trees without written authorization.

2. Arborist sign-off

Protect high-value shade/native trees and inspect union structure. No grafts over busy paths where breakage or falling fruit adds risk.

3. Ecological fit

Screen locally invasive species, biosecurity and native habitats. Tree food production is not a license to replace useful canopy.

4. Equity & access

Co-design with nearby residents and existing stewards; specify free/public access, sharing protocols and cultural priorities.

5. Health & water

Check site-history, pollution, soil/fruit risks, pesticide use, water source and safe handling. Fruiting crops can still be contaminated.

6. Continuing responsibility

Budget pruning, crop collection, cleanup, pest management and remediation. An unguided graft is not a food-security intervention.

Recommended municipal pilot protocol

  1. Publish an accessible call for sites and obtain neighborhood consent.
  2. Inventory existing tree species, ownership, condition and ecological services.
  3. Select 5–20 candidate trees/sites for independent horticultural assessment (not a target graft quota).
  4. Perform soil-history and plant-health review; use local nursery certified scions.
  5. Approve a tightly bounded pilot and publish maintenance commitments.
  6. Measure survival, union quality, harvested edible mass, cleanup hours, costs, equity and adverse effects over 3+ seasons.
  7. Stop or revise when harms outweigh benefits; publish failures, not just wins.

Policy template · editable

“The city may evaluate volunteer-proposed edible tree grafts on city-owned trees only following species verification, written arborist authorization, plant-health review, community consultation, accessibility assessment, and funded long-term maintenance. No automatic approval is implied.”

Zero-harm · Anti-inversion. No unauthorized cuts, forced sharing, ecological introductions or unsupported claims. This is a planning archive, not a live tree survey or planting permit.
