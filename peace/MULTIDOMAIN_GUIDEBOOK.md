# SOL Peace Commons — Multidomain Guidebook 2.4

**As of 2026-10-15.** Independent public-interest education; not endorsed by any government or UN body.

These guides explain *what a public researcher can ask*, *what evidence is necessary*, and *when an authorized institution is the decision owner*. They do not provide military targeting, offensive cyber, covert collection, private-person surveillance, or legal enforcement access.

## Five guided learning perspectives

## USSS · Financial integrity
Trace documented payment risks without treating every commercial transaction as a war crime.
**Mandate boundary:** No bank access, covert financial surveillance, criminal adjudication or export-control authority assumed.
**Possible deliverable:** Public financial-exposure matrix, procedural-status summary, corrective compliance question.

### Mission in one minute
**Simple:** A public-interest researcher can study cyber-enabled financial crimes and payment-system integrity without acting as an investigator.
**Advanced:** USSS Cyber Fraud Task Forces address defined financial and cyber-enabled offenses; public archive work is not a basis to access private banking or identity data.
**Try:** Write one sentence identifying the potentially relevant financial-crime behavior; state the legal question rather than asserting guilt.
**Check:** Can the described activity be distinguished from a lawful commercial payment?
**Avoid:** Overstating jurisdiction from a sanctions or export allegation alone.

### Understand the lawful boundary
**Simple:** Financial integrity is the lens, not secret intelligence or war policy.
**Advanced:** A payment involving Russia is not automatically fraudulent or forbidden. Distinguish payer, payee, beneficial owner, reason for payment, applicable sanctions, and evidence of concealment.
**Try:** Sketch a five-field public payment flow without private account identifiers.
**Check:** Is there an actual suspected financial offense and a competent referral authority?
**Avoid:** Collecting bank account numbers, private addresses, or inferred criminal intent.

### Build a dated source trail
**Simple:** Start with public regulatory notices, court records and corporate statements.
**Advanced:** Record publication date, period covered, exact conduct alleged, sanctions designation versus final conviction, and whether rebuttals exist.
**Try:** Add one dated government notice and one corroborating independent publication.
**Check:** Could another reviewer reproduce the conclusion from those sources?
**Avoid:** Treating a sanctions listing as a criminal verdict.

### Disambiguate corporate entities
**Simple:** Check which legal entity appears in each document.
**Advanced:** Match names against jurisdiction, registration, subsidiaries and ownership changes; do not collapse affiliates or similarly named firms into one legal person.
**Try:** Create manufacturer / exporter / intermediary / importer columns and mark missing links.
**Check:** Do corporate relationships come from documented filings rather than names alone?
**Avoid:** Assigning all subsidiary conduct to a parent without evidence.

### Construct a non-sensitive transaction timeline
**Simple:** Sequence publicly documented events before drawing causation.
**Advanced:** Separate contract date, export date, payment date, delivery and enforcement action; record uncertainty when only ranges are available.
**Try:** Draw a three-event timeline from a court filing or regulation.
**Check:** Can each date be traced to its source and timezone where material?
**Avoid:** Inventing unknown payment pathways.

### Identify evidence of knowledge
**Simple:** Knowledge is a separate legal and factual question.
**Advanced:** Look for adjudicated admissions, falsified end-user certificates, evasive routing described in filings and court-tested communications, while tracking exculpatory evidence.
**Try:** Classify two examples as allegation, official administrative finding or adjudicated criminal fact.
**Check:** Does cited evidence establish knowing conduct or merely financial proximity?
**Avoid:** Assuming that discovering a component proves manufacturer complicity.

### Choose the appropriate authority
**Simple:** Referral ownership depends on the activity and law.
**Advanced:** A financial-fraud concern may involve USSS and prosecutors; export licensing/enforcement generally implicates BIS, HSI or other competent agencies. Coordinate only through existing lawful channels.
**Try:** Draft a short public-source routing table with two possible recipient types.
**Check:** Have you avoided claiming that a public-interest prototype can submit a government case?
**Avoid:** Presenting an unofficial project as a Secret Service task force.

### Design a proportional safeguard
**Simple:** Protect lawful commerce and innocent parties.
**Advanced:** Recommend distributor due diligence, payment-screening quality assurance and record retention before sweeping payment restrictions; ask counsel about legal bases and exceptions.
**Try:** Draft one bounded intervention with civilian cost, reversal trigger and independent outcome.
**Check:** Would a false positive freeze legitimate humanitarian or civilian funds?
**Avoid:** Financial exclusion without authority or due process.

### Collaborate without leaking data
**Simple:** Share only the minimum public facts required for independent review.
**Advanced:** Use recipient-specific public-only JSON with source URLs, confidence, limitations and an explicit absence of agency endorsement. No personal identifiers, covert communications or live institutional link.
**Try:** Prepare a hypothetical public-only handoff and inspect each field.
**Check:** Can the receiver evaluate the issue without sensitive facts?
**Avoid:** Auto-sharing local or peer data because Trystero is connected.

### Measure outcomes and review errors
**Simple:** A better metric is lawful, documented correction—not the number of accusations.
**Advanced:** Track verified filing corrections, screened transactions reviewed by authorized compliance teams, substantiated referrals and false-positive reversals.
**Try:** Write a metric, one warning trigger and a stop rule.
**Check:** Would measurement reward accuracy and civilian safety rather than volume?
**Avoid:** Treating a proposal as successful enforcement.

## FBI · Lawful case evidence
Keep legal proceedings, manufacturer origin, exporter identity and proof of knowledge separate.
**Mandate boundary:** No investigations, suspect surveillance, private data collection or purported official referrals.
**Possible deliverable:** Public-source case chronology, proof ladder, correction and alternative explanation.

### What the FBI does
**Simple:** The FBI has investigative and intelligence functions under law; this community system has neither.
**Advanced:** Relevant public lenses include transnational procurement conspiracies, economic espionage, cyber incidents and federal criminal process. Roles overlap with DOJ, Commerce BIS and HSI.
**Try:** Choose one public case and identify exactly what law and institution it involved.
**Check:** Are you describing public education rather than opening a private investigation?
**Avoid:** Confusing a research index with a law-enforcement case system.

### Legal stage and presumption
**Simple:** A charge is not a conviction.
**Advanced:** Distinguish complaint, indictment, guilty plea, verdict, sentencing and administrative sanctions. State what is known about the evidentiary burden in each source.
**Try:** Mark three casebook claims by procedural posture.
**Check:** Can a defendant rebuttal or subsequent outcome change the phrasing?
**Avoid:** Identifying someone as guilty based solely on an allegation.

### Component origin versus end use
**Simple:** Manufacturer origin and knowing illicit export are different claims.
**Advanced:** Use manufacturer identity, product model, export documentation, consignee and unit-level provenance separately; never convert same-model shipment into serial-level proof.
**Try:** Build a five-link evidence chain and leave unidentified links explicitly blank.
**Check:** Which links are documented versus inferred?
**Avoid:** Equating product origin with knowledge of prohibited transfers.

### Open-source case chronology
**Simple:** Public dockets and press releases require context.
**Advanced:** Record charged time period, alleged mechanism, jurisdiction, cooperating agencies and stated limits; dates of publication may postdate the conduct.
**Try:** Create a citation-first chronology with one counter-source or defense statement.
**Check:** Could a reviewer distinguish the crime date from the reporting date?
**Avoid:** Cherry-picking prosecutors’ allegations as adjudicated facts.

### Export control crosswalk
**Simple:** Not all suspected technology diversion belongs to the FBI alone.
**Advanced:** BIS administers export controls; criminal investigations and prosecutions can involve FBI, HSI, DOJ and other partners. Any concrete compliance action requires professional legal review.
**Try:** Write a hypothetical handoff with separate control, investigation and prosecution owners.
**Check:** Does the action rest within the identified authority?
**Avoid:** Conflating regulatory enforcement, national intelligence and civilian policing.

### Protect evidence integrity
**Simple:** Preservation and authenticity are separate from guilt.
**Advanced:** Use original URLs, archived date, file SHA-256 and preservation notes. Hashes establish file consistency, not truth, authorship or chain of custody by themselves.
**Try:** Hash an authorized local public document and record context.
**Check:** Do you know who obtained the material and when?
**Avoid:** Handling stolen, classified, or private evidence inside a public peer room.

### Cyber incident boundaries
**Simple:** Support reporting and defense only.
**Advanced:** Public guidance can address secure backups, access control, phishing resilience and documenting observable harm. Do not probe targets, intercept traffic or trace private individuals.
**Try:** Develop a lawful defensive incident checklist for a public institution.
**Check:** Are all recommended measures defensive and consent-based?
**Avoid:** Turning an incident review into intrusion or surveillance instructions.

### Minimize privacy harms
**Simple:** Better evidence should not require more unnecessary personal data.
**Advanced:** Remove names of private persons, banking details, unneeded device identifiers and exact civilian whereabouts. Public court names still deserve contextual, non-defamatory treatment.
**Try:** Redact a sample public handoff and note justified inclusions.
**Check:** Could the same analytical result be achieved with less data?
**Avoid:** Publishing witness information or sensitive personal addresses.

### Referrals and reciprocal review
**Simple:** Propose routing; never simulate official submission.
**Advanced:** Offer a concise public-source memo with issue, sources, legal-status classification, limitations, competent authorities and steps requiring authorized investigators.
**Try:** Prepare a two-paragraph public referral draft for independent professional review.
**Check:** Does the draft clearly state it was not sent?
**Avoid:** Faking agency credentials or confirmation.

### Correct, withdraw, and audit
**Simple:** A corrected record is evidence of quality.
**Advanced:** Track challenges, source corrections, acquittals, withdrawn allegations and record retractions. Disclose when a prior inference must change.
**Try:** Revise one hypothetical mistaken claim and keep its correction history.
**Check:** Can the system reverse a harmful assertion quickly and transparently?
**Avoid:** Preserving erroneous accusations for gamified XP.

## CIA · Competing foreign explanations
Explore alternative hypotheses, confidence, source gaps and diplomatic implications.
**Mandate boundary:** No clandestine collection, foreign influence operations, covert action or policy authority.
**Possible deliverable:** Dated analytic note, competing hypotheses, disconfirming evidence and confidence rationale.

### Foreign analysis, not law enforcement
**Simple:** This module models disciplined foreign-source analysis. It is not the CIA or a covert tool.
**Advanced:** CIA produces foreign-intelligence analysis for decision-makers and does not itself make policy. No collection tradecraft, clandestine methods or domestic enforcement are provided.
**Try:** Describe a foreign-policy question in neutral, answerable terms.
**Check:** Have you avoided speaking as though the system is an official agency?
**Avoid:** Using analysis claims to justify covert action.

### Define the question and baseline
**Simple:** A precise question prevents vague speculation.
**Advanced:** Specify geography, period, actors, known facts, assumptions and how the answer could change a lawful civilian-protection or diplomacy decision.
**Try:** Rewrite “Why does war continue?” into a narrowly testable observation question.
**Check:** What dated evidence would answer it?
**Avoid:** Treating broad narratives as evidence.

### Separate source types
**Simple:** A statement by a party is a fact about what was said, not proof the claim is true.
**Advanced:** Tag VERIFIED RECORD, PARTY CLAIM, INFERENCE, LEGAL FINDING and UNKNOWN; distinguish official institutional views from independently corroborated observations.
**Try:** Grade three claims and write one sentence of uncertainty for each.
**Check:** Can the label be justified without relying on author reputation alone?
**Avoid:** Conflating government position with objective fact.

### Competing hypotheses
**Simple:** Never stop at the first plausible explanation.
**Advanced:** Construct at least two causal models—security dilemma, territorial ambitions, economic constraints, political incentives—and list discriminating signals and inconvenient facts for each.
**Try:** Write two models of a stalled negotiation and one falsifier per model.
**Check:** Would the second model change your recommendation if verified?
**Avoid:** Inventing hidden motives or confidential intelligence.

### Timeline and base rates
**Simple:** Temporal order does not automatically imply causation.
**Advanced:** Mark when assistance, attacks and announcements occurred; test whether changes preceded claimed effects and what alternative drivers exist. Do not invent event probabilities.
**Try:** Build a before/after timeline and list confounders.
**Check:** Can the analysis distinguish coincidence from an observed mechanism?
**Avoid:** Presenting correlation as intention.

### Confidence calibration
**Simple:** Explain why confidence is limited.
**Advanced:** Confidence should reflect independent corroboration, source proximity, alternative explanations and evidence gaps—not an arbitrary numeric probability.
**Try:** Write a high/medium/low confidence note with reasons and future tests.
**Check:** Is the confidence label consistent with the evidence?
**Avoid:** Showing spurious “93% certainty” for political intentions.

### Early warning with stop rules
**Simple:** Focus on observable civilian-risk indicators.
**Advanced:** Look for public infrastructure incident records, humanitarian access changes, negotiation declarations and verifiable compliance. Avoid location-specific targeting or real-time combat intelligence.
**Try:** Choose three non-operational early-warning indicators and reporting cadence.
**Check:** Would reporting reveal protected civilian movements or humanitarian vulnerabilities?
**Avoid:** Publishing sensitive operational location information.

### Structured dissent
**Simple:** Dissent strengthens a finding when grounded in evidence.
**Advanced:** Write strongest alternative interpretation, its supporting source, and a clear observation that could reverse the finding. Encourage corrections even when a team agreed.
**Try:** Author one analytic dissent memo of 150 words.
**Check:** Could a reader understand why credible people disagree?
**Avoid:** Rewarding confidence or consensus instead of corrections.

### Bridge foreign analysis to diplomacy
**Simple:** Assessment does not authorize negotiation.
**Advanced:** Translate findings into conditional policy options for authorized decision-makers: action, independent verification, reciprocal response and civilian safeguard; mark decisions as external.
**Try:** Create three non-coercive diplomatic policy options with reversible steps.
**Check:** Does the summary preserve Ukraine’s sovereignty and negotiating agency?
**Avoid:** Suggesting secret bargains made by the prototype.

### Review and expiration
**Simple:** Every finding should have an “expires when” rule.
**Advanced:** Attach an as-of date and update triggers, archive obsolete assumptions and track what changed. Reassess after talks, new casualty data or substantiated contradictory records.
**Try:** Draft a correction trigger for one analysis.
**Check:** Could someone distinguish old analysis from current verified status?
**Avoid:** Treating October 2026 material as perpetually live.

## USMC · Civilian protection
Review defense policy, harm prevention, non-operational ceasefire safeguards and recovery.
**Mandate boundary:** No military command, target selection, weapons employment, troop movements or offensive cyber.
**Possible deliverable:** Civilian-harm review, protective contingency, monitored compliance requirement.

### Policy lens and legal remit
**Simple:** This is a civilian-protection learning lens, not military command software.
**Advanced:** Study U.S. defense-policy records, lawful security assistance, end-use accountability and DoD civilian-harm standards. It cannot issue orders or military recommendations for strikes.
**Try:** Summarize a published civilian-harm policy in ordinary language.
**Check:** Does the output avoid tactical targeting or weapons instructions?
**Avoid:** Claiming the prototype controls U.S. forces.

### Distinguish defense from aggression
**Simple:** Weapons supply and war prolongation are not synonymous.
**Advanced:** Separate lawful support for self-defense from illegal attacks and knowing facilitation of violations. Evaluate transfer conditions, recipient, end-use and observed behavior, not assumed motive.
**Try:** Build a two-column legal-purpose and observed-end-use table.
**Check:** What primary evidence supports each conclusion?
**Avoid:** Treating all defense assistance as deliberate escalation.

### Civilian harm assessment
**Simple:** Start with people, not equipment.
**Advanced:** Use public reports of deaths, injuries and damage; respect reporting gaps and avoid locations that expose civilians. Assess accessible shelter, health, water and energy effects.
**Try:** Choose a public harm metric and describe undercount risk.
**Check:** Can it be measured without identifying vulnerable people?
**Avoid:** Using real-time strike data or protected coordinates.

### End-use and procurement assurance
**Simple:** Legitimate procurement can still require accountability.
**Advanced:** Record contracting authority, export permission, intended use, monitoring provisions and civilian safeguards. A public researcher can propose audit questions but cannot conduct classified inspections.
**Try:** Write a lawful procurement-accountability checklist with missing-data fields.
**Check:** Have parties’ independent legal responsibilities remained distinct?
**Avoid:** Publishing sensitive procurement routes.

### Critical infrastructure resilience
**Simple:** Emphasize defensive continuity planning.
**Advanced:** Plan backup power, water continuity, medical supply redundancy and disaster recovery at non-sensitive planning levels. Favor civilian-led needs assessments.
**Try:** Outline a city-level service-resilience concept without exposing facility vulnerabilities.
**Check:** Can the same result be achieved without identifying targets?
**Avoid:** Detailed weak-point mapping of critical systems.

### Limited ceasefires and safety
**Simple:** A ceasefire proposal needs consent and independent checks.
**Advanced:** Define humanitarian scope, civilian-protection objective, incident reporting, independent adjudication and remedies, without supplying operational battlefield deployment details.
**Try:** Draft a non-operational energy-infrastructure truce checklist.
**Check:** What happens if one incident is disputed?
**Avoid:** Treating an announced truce as implemented.

### Incident evidence separation
**Simple:** Observation is not a final legal finding.
**Advanced:** Classify reported civilian harm, authenticated imagery, party claims, legal review and independent monitoring separately. Protect witnesses and avoid public chains that identify survivors.
**Try:** Mark three hypothetical incident records as confirmed, claimed or unresolved.
**Check:** Would protected persons be at risk if this record became public?
**Avoid:** Publishing personally identifiable witness information.

### Escalation premortem
**Simple:** Design bounded-downside interventions.
**Advanced:** Review whether monitoring signals can be spoofed, ceasefire provisions exploited, civilian relief politicized or third parties misunderstood; define a withdrawal trigger.
**Try:** Write a 24-month failure story then revise one safeguard.
**Check:** Would the fallback protect civilians if consensus collapses?
**Avoid:** Optimizing military outcomes over protection.

### Institutional handoff limits
**Simple:** No single institution owns all pieces of peace.
**Advanced:** Differentiate DoD civilian harm policy, DOJ enforcement, UN humanitarian mandates, independent civil society and Ukrainian consent; avoid informal transfers of sensitive data.
**Try:** Draw a public responsibility matrix with consent gates.
**Check:** Is every proposed recipient legally and practically appropriate?
**Avoid:** Equating a research comparison with military coordination.

### Reconstruction and recovery
**Simple:** Durability means functioning services after hostilities.
**Advanced:** Pair civilian resilience with local governance, technical standards, maintenance financing, independent audits and equitable access to benefits.
**Try:** Draft a recovery outcome metric and an independent audit step.
**Check:** Will benefits remain after donor funding ends?
**Avoid:** Civilian dependency and unmeasured environmental harm.

## UN · Humanitarian assurance
Center civilian needs, legal mandates, consent, impartial monitoring and durable recovery.
**Mandate boundary:** No automatic UN mission deployment, mandate, negotiation or access to protected humanitarian data.
**Possible deliverable:** Needs-based safeguard, consent/mandate gate, anonymized outcome metric.

### UN ecosystem, not one office
**Simple:** The UN includes distinct bodies and mandates.
**Advanced:** Differentiate OHCHR human-rights monitoring, OCHA humanitarian coordination, UNDP development, UNICEF child protection, Security Council mandates and peacekeeping missions.
**Try:** Match a proposed civilian need to an appropriate UN function.
**Check:** Is the specific body named rather than “the UN” as a monolith?
**Avoid:** Pretending the project can dispatch a UN mission.

### Charter principles and sovereignty
**Simple:** Peace work begins with law and consent.
**Advanced:** Respect the UN Charter, sovereignty, territorial integrity, human rights and international humanitarian law; distinguish de facto control from de jure recognition.
**Try:** Write the governing legal principle and note contested interpretations.
**Check:** Does a proposal avoid forced transfers or coerced territorial recognition?
**Avoid:** False moral equivalence about invasion and lawful defense.

### Humanitarian independence
**Simple:** Aid must serve people, not bargaining leverage.
**Advanced:** Apply humanity, neutrality, impartiality and operational independence appropriate to humanitarian actors. Do not make essential assistance conditional on political concessions.
**Try:** Design a needs-based distribution safeguard.
**Check:** Can relief reach civilians without exposing recipients?
**Avoid:** Turning beneficiary records into intelligence or leverage.

### Civilian casualty evidence
**Simple:** Document harm with dates and uncertainty.
**Advanced:** Use OHCHR reports and clearly separate verified minimum figures from alleged or likely larger totals; protect survivors, locations and witness identity.
**Try:** Write an accurately dated casualty indicator and its limitations.
**Check:** Could the number be safely audited and updated?
**Avoid:** Treating unverified claims as total verified casualties.

### Negotiation readiness
**Simple:** Consent-dependent actions cannot be initiated by an app.
**Advanced:** Clarify who can consent, who can monitor, which issues can be decoupled, and what the legal mandate would cover; protect Ukrainian agency and civilian participants.
**Try:** Make a consent checklist for a humanitarian access arrangement.
**Check:** Is it a proposal, a party claim or a signed and operating agreement?
**Avoid:** Claiming a peace agreement exists from an announcement.

### Monitoring mission authority
**Simple:** UN peacekeeping requires authorization and practical consent.
**Advanced:** Traditional principles include consent, impartiality and non-use of force except self-defense and defense of mandate. Security Council authorization, deployment and host acceptance cannot be assumed.
**Try:** Map what would have to be formally authorized for observers.
**Check:** Would this proposal still work if UN approval is blocked?
**Avoid:** Presenting a “UN force” as already active.

### Ceasefire incident reporting
**Simple:** Use a neutral, protective reporting standard.
**Advanced:** Define what counts as a report, minimum evidence, incident timestamp, independent reviewer, disputed status, safe disclosure and correction window.
**Try:** Create a fictional incident form without coordinates or protected identities.
**Check:** Can the parties contest a report through due process?
**Avoid:** Posting raw testimony publicly.

### POWs, missing people and children
**Simple:** Humanitarian cases require confidentiality and specialized mandates.
**Advanced:** Protect family privacy and use recognized humanitarian channels for tracing, detention and return issues. No crowdsourced person-tracking or location posting.
**Try:** Propose anonymized aggregate progress indicators.
**Check:** Are sensitive records kept out of peer rooms and public JSON?
**Avoid:** Exposing detainees, children, or displaced families.

### Recovery and reparations
**Simple:** Durable peace needs community agency.
**Advanced:** Prioritize water, health, shelter, education, demining and locally accountable procurement; separate needs estimates from funds committed, disbursed and spent.
**Try:** Draft a locally governed reconstruction monitoring concept.
**Check:** What independent community feedback can be measured?
**Avoid:** Large donor-led plans without local consent or maintenance.

### Peace durability and dissent
**Simple:** A truce is not the same as a durable settlement.
**Advanced:** Evaluate independent verification, compliance remedies, justice pathways, security commitments and survivor trust; preserve disagreement without abandoning fact standards.
**Try:** Compare a sectoral truce and a general armistice across five gates.
**Check:** Does the design survive a political or funding shock?
**Avoid:** Declaring success solely because fighting paused briefly.

## Ten mission templates

### Protect civilian services
Keep water, health, food and energy service access measurable without identifying vulnerable facilities.
**Core lenses:** un, usmc, cia
**Starting records:** OHCHR / OCHA aggregate reports
**Outcome:** Public harm metric; community consent; independent reporting; stop if disclosure increases risk.

### Document restricted component diversion
Separate manufacturer, exporter, intermediary and knowledge of unlawful transfer.
**Core lenses:** fbi, usss, cia
**Starting records:** Court records; corporate filings; BIS guidance
**Outcome:** Five-link evidence chain; legal posture; non-accusatory public notice; corrected false positives.

### Assess a proposed sectoral ceasefire
Test whether an energy or humanitarian pause has consent, defined scope, monitors and remedies.
**Core lenses:** un, usmc, cia
**Starting records:** Party statements and independent observer reports
**Outcome:** Consent matrix; public verified-incident aggregate; dispute mechanism; fallback.

### Map energy revenue and civilian costs
Evaluate commercial trade links alongside legal relief exemptions and civilian effects.
**Core lenses:** usss, cia, un
**Starting records:** Independent energy trade research; official sanctions
**Outcome:** Transaction categories; confidence notes; civilian cost; legal review.

### Defend civilian digital continuity
Develop consent-based cyber resilience for relief organizations and public-service providers.
**Core lenses:** fbi, usss, un
**Starting records:** Public defensive guidance and incident reports
**Outcome:** Offline backup plan; incident-notification tree; no offensive probing.

### Rebuild services with community governance
Compare sustainable recovery options and independent outcomes over years.
**Core lenses:** un, usmc, usss
**Starting records:** World Bank, UN, public procurement reporting
**Outcome:** Local ownership; lifecycle costs; audits; equity safeguards.

### Protect POW and missing-person processes
Safely track aggregate humanitarian progress while keeping family records private.
**Core lenses:** un, cia
**Starting records:** ICRC and UN public reporting
**Outcome:** Aggregate indicator; confidentiality review; no crowdsourced person tracking.

### Test competing narratives fairly
Identify which claims are independently supported and which would change the conclusions.
**Core lenses:** cia, fbi, un
**Starting records:** Court records, UN records and primary public statements
**Outcome:** Two hypotheses; source matrix; corrections; protected identities.

### Improve public financial transparency
Target verifiable illicit behavior without penalizing lawful aid or commerce.
**Core lenses:** usss, fbi, un
**Starting records:** Regulator guidance; public enforcement actions
**Outcome:** Evidence-graded compliance memo; humanitarian exemption test; stop rule.

### Red-team a 24-month peace failure
Challenge plausible agreements for noncompliance, spoilers, rearmament and civilian harm.
**Core lenses:** cia, usmc, un
**Starting records:** Published verification doctrine; humanitarian indicators
**Outcome:** Failure story; independent metric; reversal trigger; protection fallback.
