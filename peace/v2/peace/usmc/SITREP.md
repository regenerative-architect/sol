## Actors and boundaries

Ukraine and affected communities: sovereign authority, consent, civilian agency, self-defense and human rights. Russia: state responsible for the full-scale invasion; sanctions and procurement investigations must establish specific parties and conduct. U.S. DoD: defense assistance policy and civilian harm prevention subject to U.S. authority; not domestic police. State, Treasury, Commerce, HSI, FBI and DOJ: institutionally distinct diplomacy, licensing, sanctions, enforcement and judicial functions. UN/ICRC: independent diplomatic/humanitarian mandates; ICRC must not be used as an intelligence collection channel.

## Highest-value nonviolent public policy tests

1. Preserve and publish accurate, dated civilian-harm reporting without identifying vulnerable locations or private persons.
2. Test lawful end-use accountability without treating self-defense as aggression.
3. Evaluate a limited, reciprocal civilian infrastructure non-attack arrangement with accepted independent verification.
4. Define incident classification, rebuttal, proportionality and rapid de-escalation mechanisms before discussing major concessions.
5. Design rights-centered reconstruction metrics and security safeguards that prevent renewed aggression.

**Limitations:** No live feeds, force positions, precise infrastructure coordinates, tactical guidance, sanctions evasion instructions, official interagency connectivity or authenticated evidence intake.
