# UN Peace Assurance OS — Dated SITREP (14 October 2026)

**Status:** independent public-source prototype. Not a UN report, endorsement, incident-monitoring feed or peacekeeping deployment. Facts are date frozen.

## Executive summary
UN Human Rights Monitoring Mission in Ukraine reports **at least 372 civilians killed and 2,349 injured in August 2026**. Cumulative January–August 2026 figures in its September 16 report are **2,222 killed and 13,058 injured**. These are minimum verified figures, not the full toll or October live counts. [OHCHR](https://ukraine.ohchr.org/en/Protection-of-Civilians-in-Armed-Conflict-August-2026).

On 9 October, UN Human Rights High Commissioner Volker Türk condemned reported lethal civilian strikes and widespread electricity disruptions, citing some figures from local authorities. [OHCHR official statement](https://ukraine.ohchr.org/en/Comment-by-UN-Human-Rights-Chief-Volker-T%C3%BCrk-on-deadly-strikes-in-Ukraine). A Reuters report dated 14 October described additional attacks and civilian impact; third-party reporting remains attributed, not identical to UN verified minimums. [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/russian-strikes-kill-one-hit-energy-facilities-kyiv-mayor-says-2026-10-14/).

The 2026 humanitarian response plan estimated **10.8 million people in need**, targeted **4.1 million**, and appealed for **US$2.3 billion**. The separate October 2026–March 2027 winter response plan targets **2.1 million** people and seeks **US$358.4 million**. Neither appeal amount is treated as received funding. [UN HNRP](https://ukraine.un.org/en/308290-relentless-war-ukraine-grinds-humanitarian-partners-aim-reach-41-million-people-2026) · [Winter plan](https://ukraine.un.org/en/319798-humanitarian-community-launches-winter-response-plan-help-21-million-people-across-ukraine).

## Binding obstacles (not quantified rankings)
Territorial sovereignty and unlawful aggression; security commitments; expected bargaining leverage; consent and operational access; impartial incident verification; civilian protection and health/energy access; accountability and reparations; sustainable recovery financing. These are analytical mechanisms, not claims of secret intent.

## Four distinct portfolios
1. Humanitarian confidence-building: medical access, tracing, winter relief. Consent and neutrality; independent delivery checks.
2. Limited sectoral ceasefires: signed time-bounded energy, medical and water protection; authenticated terms and adjudication.
3. General ceasefire / armistice: legally authorized verification mechanism with political consent, impartial evidence and protection; no de jure territorial concession inferred from temporary lines.
4. Durable security, justice and reconstruction: credible guarantees, civilian reparations, transparent procurement, locally governed restoration and voluntary safe return.

## Immediate measures that do not presume consent to a new UN deployment
Publish dated public data corrections; audit public winter funding gaps; convene public expert briefings under lawful permissions; support independent humanitarian access through genuine authorities; document reported violations as reports pending corroboration.

## Unknowns and next tests
Authentic signed 2026 bilateral ceasefire text; consent to any monitoring mission; latest OHCHR monthly casualty report beyond August; current funding realized and beneficiary reach; secure access in occupied territory; aggregated missing-person and POW updates; enforcement of any lasting security guarantees.

## Standards
Follow the UN Charter, IHL and humanitarian independence. Do not equate Ukrainian self-defense with Russia's invasion. Do not imply that an announced truce is implemented or that a temporary line legally transfers sovereignty. Do not collect protected information or deploy monitors from this software.
