# Civilian Protection and Durable Peace — Illustrative Nonviolent Runbook

**Status:** proposals only. **No automatic action.** Intended for public-interest educational review and authorized policy professionals with competent legal oversight.

## 0–72 hours

**Owner:** public research and humanitarian analysts. **Ask:** validate and timestamp OHCHR civilian-impact baseline, list urgent aggregate service needs, correct source errors. **Mechanism:** shared facts and reduced disinformation. **Cost:** low to moderate research effort (not independently estimated). **Dependency:** source rights, privacy, no classified inputs. **Milestone:** signed-off source ledger with separate fact, party claim, hypothesis, and unknown. **Stop:** remove unverified assertions and confidential details. **Fallback:** publish neutral methodological caveats.

## 2–6 weeks

**Owner:** authorized agency policy teams, acquisition counsel, corporate compliance, humanitarian experts. **Ask:** review end-use oversight, public support delivery accountability and civilian-service resilience plans. **Mechanism:** reduce diversion/false allegations and harmful outages. **Cost:** administrative to substantial according to scope. **Dependency:** competent authority, cross-institutional consent and independent humanitarian protections. **Milestone:** gap register and non-sensitive public remedies. **Stop:** audit/consultation risks criminalizing lawful defense, exposing civilians or obstructing essential goods. **Fallback:** restrict review to verified public evidence and legal advice.

## 2–6 months

**Owner:** authorized diplomats and independently mandated monitors with parties' consent. **Ask:** formulate a testable reciprocal sectoral ceasefire (e.g., civilian energy access). **Mechanism:** measurable confidence and civilian harm reduction. **Cost:** monitor security and training dependent. **Dependency:** written consent, incident thresholds, rebuttal and verification access. **Milestone:** defined protected category, independent review timeframes, published aggregate compliance. **Stop:** impartial monitoring denied or confidentiality compromised. **Fallback:** maintain humanitarian minimums and hotlines.

## 6–24 months

**Owner:** parties and legitimate external guarantors. **Ask:** negotiate monitored general cessation and robust security guarantees. **Mechanism:** reduce incentives for renewal and build durable observability. **Cost:** high, not reliably estimable here. **Dependency:** sovereign consent, acceptable commitments, legal boundaries, independent correction and proportional responses. **Milestone:** verified reductions in hostilities plus retained civilian access. **Stop:** unacceptable erosion of sovereignty, repeated material unaddressed breaches or imminent civilian harm. **Fallback:** narrower monitored protections and diplomatic mechanisms.

## 2–10 years

**Owner:** Ukraine, affected communities and audited reconstruction institutions. **Ask:** repair infrastructure, restore environmental function and expand transparent civilian-led recovery. **Mechanism:** improve non-recurrence and community agency. **Cost:** very high/multiyear. **Dependency:** demining safety, procurement integrity, consultation and rights protections. **Milestone:** verified utility uptime, safe return, restored schools/health access, independent audit findings. **Stop:** forced transfer, conflicts of interest, environmental harms, unsafe returns. **Fallback:** independent civilian relief and corrected funding conditions.

## Model verification rules

1. Validate mandates and voluntary party agreement before any official observer role.
2. Collect **only authorized** aggregate evidence, protect witness identities, do not publicize precise sensitive locations.
3. Acknowledge an accepted incident report within 24 hours (design target), review within 72 hours when possible (design target), publish unresolved disputes as unresolved.
4. Require second-source corroboration where feasible; preserve lawful alternatives and accident categories.
5. Never trigger force or economic punishment automatically. Response must be lawful, proportional, adjudicated and reversible where possible.

## 24-month failure check

Premortem: false compliance, masked rearmament, cyber compromise, exposure of protected civilian sites, improper intelligence use of neutral humanitarian data, illicit supply-chain allegations without due process, reconstruction capture, and externally imposed unacceptable political terms. Revise or stop each protocol when an observed warning invalidates its assumptions.

## Outcome indicators

UN verified civilian harm by reporting vintage; safe energy/water access; humanitarian access; aggregated POW/missing person exchanges; independently adjudicated ceasefire incidents and observer coverage; correction/false-positive rates; reconstruction audit findings; implemented negotiation milestones; civilian trust where ethically measured.
